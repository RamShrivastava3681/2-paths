4. From Invoice to Funded Asset — The Sales Cycle

4.1 Sales Invoices /app/invoices

This is where the money happens. Every invoice you create goes through a maker-checker pipeline before reaching the funding queue — ensuring accuracy and preventing fraud.

Create Invoice → Checker Reviews → Approved → Funded → Cash in Account
Client benefit: "You submit an invoice in the morning, a checker reviews it by lunch, and the treasury funds it the same day. What used to take your bank 3 weeks now takes 11 hours."

What you can do on this page:

  • Create invoices with full line items — quantity, price, discount %, GST, freight — all calculated automatically
  • Link to the customer's sales order for automatic validation
  • Track payment status in real time: paid, overdue, advanced, partially paid, rejected
  • Send NOA (Notice of Assignment) emails with the invoice PDF attached
  • Record payments (partial or full) with late-day tracking and short-payment detection
  • Branded print preview with your company logo, colors, and bank details
  • Automated payment reminders sent to debtors on schedule (15, 7, 2, 1, 0 days before/after due)

4.2 Proforma Invoices /app/proformas

Need an advance before the final invoice? Raise a proforma against a customer's PO number. The treasury funds the advance, and it's automatically deducted when the final invoice is created.

Raise Proforma → Checker Approves → Treasury Funds Advance → Deducted from Final Invoice
  • Set an advance percentage — the system calculates the exact amount
  • Advance is tracked and deducted server-side when the final invoice is raised
  • Works for both sales (customer advances) and purchase (supplier advances)

4.3 Credit & Debit Notes /app/notes

Post-invoice adjustments, handled with the same approval rigor:

  • Credit notes: Refunds, discounts, returns — reduce what the customer owes
  • Debit notes: Extra charges, claims — increase what the customer owes
  • Every note flows through: maker → checker → applied to the linked invoice
  • Full line items with tax, document attachments, and branded print preview

5. Procurement — Know Every Dollar In and Out

5.1 Purchase Orders /app/purchase-orders

Orders placed with suppliers from your product catalogue. Every PO follows the same maker-checker discipline:

  • Pick products from the catalogue — prices auto-suggest from your most recent order
  • Send the PO to the supplier with a PDF and a secure approval link
  • Track what's been received vs. what's still pending — the system updates automatically when goods arrive
  • Status flows: draft → approved → sent → partially received → fully received

5.2 Goods Received Note (GRN) /app/grn

When goods actually arrive, you record them against the purchase order. This is the only document that puts stock on your books — no order, proforma, or invoice ever changes your inventory count.

Why this matters: "Your inventory is always accurate because only a physical goods receipt — confirmed by a real person — adds stock. No phantom numbers from pending orders."
  • Split received goods into accepted (goes to stock) and rejected (damaged/defective)
  • Barcode scan support for fast warehouse entry
  • Over-receipt protection — flagged if you receive more than ordered (admin override available)
  • Automatically updates the purchase order's received quantities

5.3 Expenses /app/expenses

Track the true cost of every deal — logistics, insurance, interest, admin costs — and link each expense to a specific sales or purchase invoice. Finally know your real per-deal economics.

5.4 Advances /app/advances

Money received from customers or paid to suppliers ahead of the final invoice. Every advance is tied to a specific invoice and automatically deducted when the final amount is settled.

How It Works (Plain Language)

The engine runs a 14-step pipeline over 12 months of your real sales history:

  1. Clean the data: If you were out of stock for part of a month, the engine scales up that month's demand to reflect what you could have sold — so stockout months don't drag your forecast down.
  2. Weight recent months more: The last 3 months count triple, the 3 before that double, the oldest 6 count once. Recent trends matter more than ancient history.
  3. Find the trend: Is demand going up, down, or flat? A statistical line of best fit tells you the direction and strength.
  4. Account for seasonality: September sells 15% more than average. January sells 35% less. The engine learns your product's "month personality."
  5. Apply business factors: Optional levers you control — trekking season, weather, promotions, regional demand, events. These nudge the forecast but can never push it beyond 70%–150% of normal.
  6. Build the 6-month forecast: Baseline × trend × seasonality × factors = your monthly prediction, with an honest 80% confidence band.
  7. Calculate reorder math: How many days of stock do you have? How many should you order? Respects your lead time, safety stock, MOQ, and order multiples.
  8. Set the timeline: When will you run out? When's the last safe day to order? When will the new stock arrive?
Example: "Trekking Backpack TB-1001: you have 30 units on hand, selling ~1 unit/day. Lead time is 14 days. The engine says you have 34 days of cover, you should reorder 41 units, and the last safe day to order is August 24th. Urgency: safe."
